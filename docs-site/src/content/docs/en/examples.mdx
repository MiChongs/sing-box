Eight configurations for common situations, ready to copy and adapt. If you are not sure where to start, get the first one running, then borrow from the others as needed. Every field is explained in [Configuration](../config/).

All examples pass `sing-box check`. Apart from the first, each lists only the Smart-related parts; take the `my-subscription` subscription, the `direct` outbound and `cache_file` from the first one and merge everything into your own configuration.

<Callout type="note" title="Adapting an example">
  `my-subscription` is the subscription's tag and `https://example.com/subscription` is a placeholder; use your own subscription. Node naming (`HK`, `Hong Kong`, `香港`) differs between providers, so fields that match names (`exclude`, `policy_priority`, region rules) must follow the names in your subscription. Matching is case-sensitive.
</Callout>

## Minimal configuration

<CodeBlock code={ex1} filename="01-minimal.json" locale="en" />

**For**: your first Smart group.

- Uses every node of one subscription, with defaults for everything else. While the group is in use, Smart checks its members every 5 minutes and learns the most reliable node for each service.
- `cache_file` keeps what was learned across restarts; without it every restart starts from zero.
- `clash_api` lets you view the ranking and pin nodes in a dashboard; remove it if you do not need it.

**Change before use**: the subscription `url`, and `update_interval` (how often the subscription refreshes) if you like.

## Tuning checks and selection

<CodeBlock code={ex2} filename="02-tuned.json" locale="en" />

**For**: a running setup where you want fewer false verdicts, a preference for some regions, or node switches that take effect immediately.

- `exclude` drops non-node entries such as traffic or expiry notices; `(?i)` makes it case-insensitive.
- `expected_status: "204"` requires the check URL to answer 204, so a captive portal (a hotel or airport Wi-Fi login page) cannot make a dead node look alive.
- `interval`, `idle_timeout` and `timeout` are written with their defaults so you can adjust them; `interval` must not exceed `idle_timeout`.
- `tolerance: 50`: fallback members outside the serving pool whose probe delays are within 50 ms keep their configured order.
- `max_failed_times: 5`: 5 errors in a row on one site block the node for that site for 24 hours.
- `policy_priority` gives nodes named "香港" or `HK` a factor of 1.2 and "日本" or `JP` 1.1, and uses a regex to lower nodes marked `x2`–`x9` (premium multipliers) to 0.7. The first matching rule wins; see [Node preferences](../scoring/#node-preferences).
- `interrupt_exist_connections: true`: changing the pin in the dashboard drops existing connections so they reconnect at once.

**Change before use**: the keywords in `exclude` to match the notice entries in your subscription; the names in `policy_priority` to match your node names.

## Enabling LightGBM

<CodeBlock code={ex3} filename="03-lightgbm.json" locale="en" />

**For**: computing connection quality with the model published by vernesong/mihomo instead of the scene formula, or collecting your own data to train a model.

- On first start `Model.bin` downloads in the background; until then the scene formula is used, so nothing breaks. `auto_update` checks for a new model every 72 hours.
- `download_detour: "direct"` downloads the model directly instead of through a Smart group that is still learning. You can use `http_client` instead; see [Configuration](../config/#lightgbm-http_client).
- `collect_data` with `sample_rate: 0.3` records about 30% of connections as training data, and stops at 100 MB.

**Change before use**: if you do not plan to train a model, remove `collect_data`, `sample_rate` and `collector`; if GitHub is unreachable directly, point `download_detour` at an outbound that can reach it, or the model `url` at a reachable mirror.

## prefer_asn and GeoX

<CodeBlock code={ex4} filename="04-asn-geox.json" locale="en" />

**For**: setups where much traffic reaches Smart through `route.final`, and sites on the same network should share what was learned.

- `prefer_asn` groups sites without a rule, or under broad rules, into one service per ASN; shared networks such as Cloudflare stay per site. See [Service keys](../service-keys/#prefer_asn).
- GeoX refreshes the ASN and country databases weekly; the ASN database serves `prefer_asn` and LightGBM, the country database LightGBM.
- The first download takes a moment; the databases are used as soon as they arrive, with no restart.

**Change before use**: remove `use_lightgbm` if you do not need LightGBM; to use the default databases only, drop the whole `geox` block and Smart downloads GeoLite2 itself.

## Routing by service

<CodeBlock code={ex5} filename="05-service-keys.json" locale="en" />

**For**: services (streaming, AI, code hosting) that should each converge on one stable exit, while other foreign sites still share experience.

- Every rule pointing at `auto` is a service: `RuleSet [geosite-netflix]`, `RuleSet [geosite-openai]` and `DomainSuffix [github.com]` each learn on their own and have their own incumbent, and all domains of one service share one exit.
- `geosite-geolocation-!cn` is a broad rule; with `prefer_asn` its sites are grouped by ASN instead of counting as one giant service.
- ASN claims attach new domains on Netflix's or OpenAI's networks, not yet listed in the rule sets, to the matching service; see [ASN claims](../service-keys/#asn-claims).
- `policy_priority` prefers private-line nodes, then Hong Kong nodes, and avoids premium-multiplier nodes.
- `sniff` gives IP-only connections a domain; `final` is `direct`, so everything else goes out directly.

**Change before use**: add or remove rule sets as you need; they download through `direct`, so switch to an outbound that works if that fails.

## Nested groups

<CodeBlock code={ex6} filename="06-nested.json" locale="en" />

**For**: many nodes, when all you care about is which region to use.

- Three `urltest` groups each pick the fastest node of one region from the subscription, and Smart learns which of the three regions to use.
- Smart treats member groups like ordinary nodes: scores are recorded under `HK`, `JP` and `US`, and reflect whichever node that group had selected at the time.
- `policy_priority: "^HK$:1.2"` uses an anchored regex so it only matches the member named `HK`; unanchored, `HK` would match any member whose name contains it.
- The outer `selector` uses `auto` by default; switch it to a fixed region in the dashboard when you need to.
- With few candidates Smart learns fast; the cost is that it cannot see the differences between nodes inside a region. To let Smart manage every node directly and spread connections within regions, use [Smart load balancing](#smart-load-balancing) below.

**Change before use**: the region keywords in `include`, to match how your nodes are named.

## Smart load balancing

<CodeBlock code={ex7} filename="07-smart-loadbalance.json" locale="en" />

**For**: a large subscription you want to hand to one group that sorts nodes by region and spreads connections across several nodes, while each site keeps a stable exit.

- Every node of the subscription goes into one `smart-loadbalance` group and is sorted into regions by name; nodes whose names contain `IPLC` or `IEPL` go to a custom region named "专线" (private line), and `deny` leaves out mainland-China nodes.
- `mode: auto` learns the best region per site, `weights` gives Hong Kong a slight edge, and `affinity: site` keeps each site on one node so logins do not break when the exit IP changes.
- `max_connections_per_node: 64` keeps any one node from taking too many concurrent connections.
- `region.outbounds` generates outbounds such as `🇭🇰 香港负载` for four regions. The group lists them as its members in dashboards (picking one locks that region), and a `selector` such as `proxy` can reference them directly.
- Details in [Smart load balancing](../smart-loadbalance/).

**Change before use**: list in `regions` only the regions your subscription actually has (the region outbounds the `selector` references must match). If a stable exit per site does not matter to you, set `affinity` back to `none` to spread the load better.

## Front of chained proxies

<CodeBlock code={ex8} filename="08-chain.json" locale="en" />

**For**: your own landing nodes (a VPS, a static residential IP and so on) reached through provider nodes, with the front connections spread over several provider nodes.

- Two landing nodes connect through provider nodes. `front` spreads the front connections over several provider nodes of the chosen region, so one throttled or failing provider node does not take every connection down.
- `landing-us` uses `front` itself: the `destination` mode picks the region by the landing server's country, and `map` sends US landing servers through US, then Japanese, fronts and UK ones through German or Dutch fronts. A landing server given by domain is resolved first.
- `landing-hk` always uses `front-HK`, the Hong Kong front. `fallback: true` lets it move to another region when every Hong Kong front is down; without it, only Hong Kong nodes would ever be tried.
- `members: "nodes"` keeps `front` listing nodes in dashboards.
- The front group keeps the default `affinity: none`; `site` or `target` would put every connection to one landing server on a single front node, which defeats the spreading.

**Change before use**: replace the two `shadowsocks` landing nodes with your own (protocol, address, port, password) and keep their `detour`; adjust `regions` and `map` to the regions in your subscription and the countries of your landing servers.
