Smart picks a node for **every single connection**. This page follows one connection in order; each step's details live in the matching guide. By design, scoring and selection follow the Score policy of [honk](https://github.com/daeuniverse/honk), while service keys, blocks, probes and convergence follow the Smart group of [vernesong/mihomo](https://github.com/vernesong/mihomo).

## Target and service

From the routing metadata Smart takes the destination domain (the domain in the destination address first, then the sniffed one), every known IP and the port, and derives keys at three levels:

| Key | Example | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Exact target | `www.example.com:443` | The finest score record (memory only) |
| Wildcard host | `*.example.com` | Block records, response probes and exit suspicions |
| Service key | `RuleSet [geosite-netflix]`, `*.example.com`, `13414` | Score, incumbent, convergence and persistence |

The wildcard host folds randomly generated subdomains together: `rr3---sn-abc.googlevideo.com` and `rr5---sn-xyz.googlevideo.com` both become `*.googlevideo.com`, and multi-label suffixes such as `example.co.uk` are handled correctly. The service key is the matched routing rule by default, else the wildcard host; with `prefer_asn`, broad rules and rule-less targets are grouped by ASN. The full rules are in [Service keys](../service-keys/).

## Filtering members

Before selection, Smart tries four increasingly loose conditions and uses the first that leaves any member:

1. Health checks pass, not blocked for the whole group, not blocked for this site or service, not deferred by an exit suspicion (and, for UDP, the member supports UDP);
2. Ignore health and group-wide blocks;
3. Ignore site and group-wide blocks, but require passing health checks;
4. Exclude only manual blocks and exit suspicions (UDP support is no longer required either).

At level 1, **one** member deferred by an exit suspicion is added back to take an 8-second "half-open" lease and test whether the suspicion still holds (see [Exit watch](../blocks-and-probes/#exit-watch)).

When more than a third of the members (at least 2) are blocked for a site with `error`, `lowWeight` or `loss`, Smart concludes the **site itself** is broken: it ignores those blocks (but not `manual`) and stops blocking more nodes over that site's failures.

## Selection

After filtering, Smart scores each member of the **serving pool** (the few members worth comparing): reliability is the payoff, latency, goodput, connection quality and loss only fine-tune it, and the result is multiplied by the `policy_priority` factor. The service's **incumbent** (the node chosen for it before) is kept unless a challenger proves a large enough gain on mature data from both sides, or the incumbent stops being reliable or has just failed. A single candidate is used directly.

The exploration budget now and then swaps the primary for a member that lacks data (cold trials, periodic trials earned by traffic, recovery trials when a failed member's backoff ends), so new and recovered nodes get compared at all. Details in [Score and selection](../scoring/).

## Dialing

The dial order is: the primary, the rest of the serving pool (by eligibility and score), then members outside the pool (not exit-suspected first, then by `policy_priority` factor and probe delay, with delays within `tolerance` keeping their configured order), at most 10 in total. Then rounds:

| Round | Members | How |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | 1st, 2nd, 3rd | One per round |
| 4 | 4th–8th | 5 race at once, the first to connect wins |
| 5 | 9th–10th | Same |

- Each round's timeout is the longest adaptive timeout of its members: the member's average connect time × 5 (at least 1 s), capped by `timeout` (5 s by default). A failure caused only by such an adaptive timeout is not held against the node.
- When the destination does not resolve (NXDOMAIN), dialing stops at once; another node would not help.
- Connections that lose a race are closed and count as neither success nor failure.
- UDP dials one member at a time; a lone member is tried 3 times.

When everything fails the error is `all members failed for <target>`; when filtering leaves nothing it is `no available member for <target>`.

## Convergence

When the primary fails and a later member connects, that member becomes the service's **incumbent**, and the service's TCP connections through other nodes are closed so clients reconnect to the same exit (mihomo's service convergence). Sites that tie logins to the exit IP therefore do not stay spread over several exits. When a node gets blocked for a service, the service's connections are closed and its incumbent is cleared too. See [Convergence](../service-keys/#convergence).

## During and after a connection

While a connection runs, Smart observes:

- **Connect time**: how long the successful dial took;
- **First response**: from the start of the dial to the first byte received;
- **Progress**: data received after sending, recorded at most once per second, proving the node still works;
- **Goodput windows**: when one direction carries 64 KiB or more within 1–10 s, an upload or download rate is recorded;
- **TCP retransmissions**: at close, the send and retransmission counters of the TCP connection to the node's server are read and judged in windows of at least 64 segments or 64 KiB (Linux, Android, macOS, FreeBSD, Windows).

The verdict at close:

| Situation | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Closed by Smart itself for convergence | Not counted |
| Not a single byte either way | Not counted |
| Read or write error from the node | Failure (for this site only) |
| Sent data, no answer for 4 s or more, on port 443 or an IP target | Failure (site level), plus a 2-minute block (`stalled`) |
| Sent data and ended before any answer (the client gave up) | Not counted |
| Anything else | Success |
| Dial failure | Failure (node level, for every site) |

A failure "for this site only" puts the node in a failure state for this site (its service and exact target) without touching its score elsewhere; a dial failure means the node itself is in trouble. Besides success or failure, Smart computes a **connection quality** with mihomo's scene formula (or the LightGBM model) and uses it to decide whether to block and whether to run a response probe; see [Blocks and probes](../blocks-and-probes/).

Connections to the health-check URL's host and connections of `block` and `dns` members are not learned.

## Manual pin

Clicking a node in the dashboard (`PUT /proxies/{group}`) pins it:

- New connections use **only** that node, with no filtering, scoring or fallback; if it cannot connect, the connection fails. A UDP connection is selected automatically when the pinned node has no UDP.
- While pinned, connection outcomes do not block nodes, so a node you chose is not blacklisted behind your back.
- The pin is saved in the cache file and restored after a restart; it is released when the node leaves the group.
- Release it with `DELETE /proxies/{group}`, a `PUT` with an empty name, or `POST /smart/groups/{group}/clear-selection`. **A group delay test (`GET /group/{group}/delay`) also releases the pin**, as in mihomo.
- With `interrupt_exist_connections`, a pin change closes existing connections.

## The current node

Smart picks a node per connection, so the dashboard's "current node" (`now`) is only a representative, taken in this order:

1. The pinned node;
2. (`smart-loadbalance`) the representative of the current region;
3. The member that carried the latest connection;
4. The member a new TCP connection would most likely get;
5. The first member.

## Background tasks

| Task | First run | Period | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health check | 10 s | `interval` | Only while the group is in use (a connection within `idle_timeout`) |
| Stability check | 5 min | 10 min | Finds jittery or slowing members from their last 10 delays; see [Health checks](../health/#stability) |
| Broken-site threshold | 1 min | 15 min | How many blocked nodes mean the site itself is broken: a third of the members, at least 2 |
| ASN claims | 1 min | 2 min | Only meaningful with `prefer_asn`; see [ASN claims](../service-keys/#asn-claims) |
| Save to the cache file | 5 min | 5 min | Also on shutdown |
| Recheck refusing sites | 15 min | 30 min | Re-probes `answer` blocks, only while in use; each round has a one-in-two chance of being skipped |
| Clean block records | 2 h | 2 h | Drops records without a failure for a week |

While the device or network is paused (a sleeping phone, for example), every task is skipped.

## Network changes

When sing-box detects a network change (switching between Wi-Fi and mobile data, for example), Smart treats every member as available again, clears the failure-flood state and, while the group is in use, runs a health check right away. Learned scores are kept.

## What Smart groups share

- **Per group**: score records, incumbents, serving pool, exploration budget, block records, exit watch and the manual pin. One node in two groups has two independent records.
- **Shared**: the health-check prober (results are reused within half an `interval`); the global response-probe limits (120 per minute, at most 4 at once); the LightGBM model and the training-data file; the ASN and country databases.
