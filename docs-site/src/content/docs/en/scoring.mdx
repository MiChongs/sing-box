Smart's scoring and selection follow the Score policy of [honk](https://github.com/daeuniverse/honk) (daeuniverse/honk). Its core idea: **reliability is the payoff, performance only breaks ties**. However fast a node is, if it clearly fails more often than others it is not chosen; only among equally reliable nodes do speed, goodput and connection quality decide. The connection quality and the node ranking come from the Smart group of vernesong/mihomo.

## Where the evidence comes from

Every connection's outcome is recorded in four layers (evidence cells) of the same node:

| Layer | Scope | Stored |
|---|---|---|
| Global | All of the node's connections in this group (TCP and UDP separate) | Cache file |
| Family | Connections to IPv4 / IPv6 destinations | Cache file |
| Service | Connections of one service key (see [Service keys](../service-keys/)) | Cache file |
| Exact target | Connections to one `host:port` | Memory only |

At selection the layers merge from coarse to fine: the more data a specific layer holds, the more it overrides its parents. A layer's weight is its useful completions ÷ 8 (at most 1), so after 8 useful connections of a service, the node's standing for that service is decided by the service layer alone; until then its global record fills in, and a new site benefits from the node's overall reputation right away.

The records are bounded (8192 service cells, 16384 exact cells, 4096 global and family cells together); when full, the least recently used are evicted.

## Reliability

In every layer each node has two decaying counts, useful successes and failures, with a **30-minute half-life**. A "useful success" is a successful connection with data in both directions; a dial failure counts extra (as 3 failures), because it says more about the node than an error after connecting. From these:

- **Observed success rate**: successes ÷ (successes + failures), 0.5 without data;
- **Reliability lower / upper bound**: the bounds of Beta(successes + 1, failures + 1) at 1.64 standard deviations. With few samples the interval is wide and only narrows with more data, so a new node with 3 successes out of 3 is not taken as more reliable than an old one with 300 successes and 1 failure.

### Failure state

A "hard failure" puts the node in a failure state: its failure streak grows by 1, it is not tried again during a backoff of 5 minutes, 10, 20 … (doubling, up to 6 hours), and its earned "proven" status is voided. A dial failure is hard in every layer; a failure after connecting (read/write errors, no answer) is hard only in the service and exact layers, so the node's standing on other sites is untouched.

When **4 different connections** in turn prove that the node receives data again (each within 60 s of the previous), the failure state clears. A single connection receiving data normally already ends the backoff early.

## Eligibility

Only "eligible" members can win an ordinary selection:

1. While other members are healthy (failure streak below 3), members with a streak of 3 or more are not eligible;
2. A member that just recovered (see above) with no new failure is eligible;
3. When some member is "proven" (4 useful completions, or normal traffic within the last 60 s), only proven members are eligible, and their upper reliability bound + 0.05 must reach the best lower bound, and their observed success rate + 0.05 the best observed rate;
4. When no member is proven, a lower bound + 0.05 reaching the best lower bound is enough.

If no member is eligible, the one with the highest lower bound is taken.

## The comprehensive score

Eligible members compete on the comprehensive score:

```text
score = observed success rate
      + 0.03 × latency term
      + 0.02 × max(upload term, download term)
      + 0.02 × quality term
      − 0.02 × loss penalty
      (then multiplied by the policy_priority factor)
```

- **The latency, upload, download and quality terms** are relative: each is compared to the best value among eligible members and lands in 0–1. Lower latency is better; higher goodput and quality are better. If only one member has data for a term, there is nothing to compare and the term is 0 for everyone.
- **The latency term** mostly reflects the first-response time (data for this service preferred); without enough response data it uses the probe delay, and without that the connect time.
- **The quality term** is mihomo's connection quality (the scene formula, or the model prediction with `use_lightgbm`); see [Connection quality](#connection-quality).
- **Loss penalty** = 1 − e^(−50 × retransmission rate), using the rate's exponential average over 30 minutes: 1% gives about 0.39, 5% about 0.92.

All performance terms together move the score by at most 0.07, while every 1% of success rate is worth 0.01. Performance therefore only matters when reliability is close.

### Freshness

Latency and goodput describe the road right now, so they age fast: each is averaged over at most the last 4 samples, confidence falls linearly once the newest sample is 60 s old, and the value is void after 120 s. Probe delays likewise count only for 2 minutes after each health check. "Mature" means 4 samples with the newest under 60 s old. Connection quality is the opposite, a long-term measure: it decays slowly with a 30-minute half-life and is kept in the cache file.

## The incumbent and switching

Every service key (TCP, UDP, IPv4 and IPv6 apart) remembers the result of its last ordinary selection, the **incumbent**. When a new connection arrives:

- The incumbent is **no longer eligible** (its reliability fell clearly behind, say): switch at once to the best-scoring member;
- The incumbent has an **unresolved failure** on this service: switch at once;
- The incumbent has hardly any data (under 0.5 completions): choose by score;
- Otherwise a challenger must prove a gain on data that is **mature on both sides**. Gain = the difference in observed success rate (when both are proven) + 0.03 × the relative improvement in response time + 0.02 × the relative improvement in goodput (counted only when one direction improves by at least 10% and neither the other direction nor the response time gets more than 10% worse). The bar is 0.005 × min(incumbent completions ÷ 8, 1).

The bar is low, but it demands mature data from both sides, so a passing fluctuation does not cause a switch while a real difference shows quickly.

### Selection reasons

`selection[].reasons` of the diagnostic endpoint `GET /smart/groups/{name}/diag` counts why each choice was made:

| Reason | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `singleton` | Only one candidate |
| `coldExplore` | A member without data tried from the cold allowance |
| `periodicExplore` | A member lacking data tried from credit earned by traffic |
| `recoveryExplore` | Recovery trial of a failed member after its backoff |
| `incumbentIneligible` | The incumbent lost eligibility and was replaced |
| `freshFailureBypass` | The incumbent just failed and was replaced |
| `insufficientEvidenceHeld` | No comparable data on both sides; incumbent kept |
| `directionalTradeoffHeld` | The challenger is faster one way and slower the other; incumbent kept |
| `incumbentHeld` | Compared, and the challenger's gain fell short; incumbent kept |
| `reliabilityWinner` | Only one member (or none) was eligible; chosen by reliability |
| `performanceWinner` | Several members were eligible and the best score won, or a challenger replaced the incumbent |

`ordinarySwitch` counts incumbent replacements, and `switchFlap` counts switches back to the previous winner within 8 ordinary selections; if the latter keeps growing, a few nodes are too close to tell apart.

## The serving pool

With many nodes, comparing all of them wastes traffic and never reaches a conclusion. Smart therefore makes ordinary choices, trials and switches inside a small **serving pool**:

- The pool grows with traffic: the more new connections per second recently, the larger it is; at least 3, at most √n rounded up plus 1 (never above 25). Groups of 3 members or fewer have everyone in the pool.
- Every 5 minutes the pool is re-ranked by each node's overall score (not per service); nodes without data are ordered by probe delay. A member is replaced only after falling 2 places below the cut, so members do not flap in and out.
- A full pool keeps one **rotation slot**: every 10 minutes another outside member takes it to get compared; once chosen it becomes a regular member.
- A member failing 3 times in a row is replaced early when a healthy outsider exists.

Members outside the pool only serve as fallbacks when the pool members cannot connect.

## Exploration

Without data there is nothing to compare, so Smart occasionally hands a connection to a member that lacks data: a **trial**. Trials are budgeted per (group, network, address family):

- **Cold allowance**: the member count up to 4 members, otherwise √n rounded up plus 1;
- **Earned credit**: every new connection earns 1/16 trial; with heavy traffic (over 2 connections per second) and mostly successful trials (80% or more), 1/8. At most 8 are banked;
- **Recovery trials**: when a failed member's backoff ends it gets a free trial;
- A member has at most one unanswered trial at a time (no result within 60 s ends it);
- When the primary works fine but trials keep failing (under 20% success), trials pause;
- Only members with fewer completions than the current choice are tried; members with enough data compete on score.

When the incumbent is replaced for losing eligibility or a fresh failure, no trial is scheduled on top. If a trial connection fails, the next member is dialed right away, so the user waits at most one extra dial round.

## Node preferences

`policy_priority` assigns factors by node name, in the same syntax as vernesong/mihomo:

```json
"policy_priority": "(?i)iplc|iepl:1.3;Hong Kong|HK:1.1;(?i)x[2-9]:0.6"
```

- Rules are separated by `;`, each `pattern:factor`, split at the last `:`; write `:` inside a pattern as `\:`.
- A pattern that compiles as a regex (RE2) matches anywhere in the name, otherwise it is a substring; matching is case-sensitive.
- **The first matching rule wins.** In the example, a Hong Kong node named `IPLC` gets 1.3, not 1.3 × 1.1.
- Factors must be above 0; a node no rule matches gets 1. Bad syntax fails startup.

What the factor does:

- **It multiplies the comprehensive score.** Scores sit around 1, so a factor of 1.1 is like a 10% success-rate advantage: a strong effect;
- But **eligibility ignores the factor**: a node clearly behind on reliability cannot win, whatever its factor;
- When the incumbent and a challenger have different factors, the difference of their scores counts directly toward the switching gain;
- Fallback members outside the serving pool are ordered by factor, highest first;
- A node whose connection quality × factor falls below 0.4 is blocked (`lowWeight`), so nodes below 1 get blocked for low quality more easily;
- The quality shown in the dashboard ranking is multiplied by the factor too. Learned quality itself is stored without it, so changing `policy_priority` takes effect at once.

<Callout type="note" title="Example">
  Two nodes, both proven on the same service: A has an observed success rate of 0.97 and answers faster (latency term 1), B has 0.99 and a latency term of 0.4, all else equal. A scores about 0.97 + 0.03 = 1.00 and B about 0.99 + 0.012 = 1.002, so B wins narrowly: reliability first. Give A a factor of 1.1 and it scores 1.10 and wins. But if A's observed rate drops to 0.80, more than 0.05 behind B, A loses eligibility and no factor can make it win.
</Callout>

## Connection quality

When a connection ends, Smart computes a quality value for it (roughly 0–1.5) with vernesong/mihomo's `CalculateWeight`:

- The connection's duration, volume and rates first identify a scene: **web**, **interactive** (games, voice; sustained traffic both ways), **streaming** (download-heavy bulk) or **transfer** (large files);
- The base score combines success rate, connect time and first-response time with scene-specific weights;
- Volume, duration, a bonus for high quality (low latency, large transfers) and a loss penalty adjust it, and it decays with the time since the previous use;
- It is not computed while the node has fewer than 2 completions on the service.

With `use_lightgbm` and a usable model, the model prediction is used instead; see [LightGBM model](../lightgbm/). The quality feeds the score's quality term, blocks the node when it falls below 0.4 (after the factor), and drives the dashboard ranking.

## The dashboard ranking

The ranking from `GET /group/{name}/weights` follows mihomo's rules:

1. Take the group's 64 most recently used services, and within each, sort the nodes by connection quality (times their factor);
2. Nodes with quality 0.4 or higher score 1, 1/2, 1/3 … by position, summed over the services;
3. `Weight` is the score as a percentage of the highest (0–100);
4. Among alive members with a positive score, the top 20% (at least 1) are `MostUsed`, the next 50% `OccasionalUsed`, and everyone else (including unavailable members) `RarelyUsed`.

Until any service has learned a quality, members are ranked by the comprehensive score. The ranking is cached for 30 s; `?refresh=true` recomputes it. It is used for display and for `smart-loadbalance` quality estimates only; it does not affect selection in a `smart` group. Fields are described in [Clash API](../clash-api/#get-groupnameweights).
